Concrete football pitch in Hythe left untouched for 26 years set for £54k transformation

A town's "unloved" and “unimaginative” concrete football pitch - left untouched for 26 years - is set for a £54,000 transformation.

The Multi-Use Games Area (MUGA) at Hythe Green was installed in 2000 with two football goals and two basketball hoops.

But over the years, the facility has become "dilapidated", with weeds growing through cracks in the ground after receiving little to no refurbishment.

Now, Hythe Town Council (HTC) has approved a scheme put forward by Cllr Stephen Bailey to completely redesign the MUGA.

Cllr Bailey told KentOnline: "I've been working on this for many years, even before I was elected, and this definitely does rank up there as one of my most successful projects.

"I established the MUGA could accommodate a five-a-side football pitch, basketball court, cricket crease and an additional activity area rather than simply housing a few poorly positioned goals and basketball hoops.

"We have the opportunity to not only resurface the concrete but to also paint it in vibrant colours and turn a grey slab into something exciting and vibrant for the community."

The ambitious project will be fully funded through the Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL), which is also being used to restore the public tennis courts in South Road.

Work is expected to begin at the end of August or the beginning of September, with construction anticipated to take about six weeks.

Cllr Bailey says he is looking forward to investing in a community space which has become "unloved" and "very unimaginative".

"The surface has been down for more than 25 years and it's never had any maintenance done to it through previous administrations so the surface is degrading but salvageable," he added.

"I was born and bred in Hythe and having children of my own made me understand the importance of having those facilities around the town.

"It's an opportunity to improve the lives of the older children in the town who are now too grown-up for the play park next to it.

"I'm really hoping Hythe Youth Club and other groups can get some use out of it too - it's got such a wonderful green space around it."

In December, KentOnline reported how residents in Hythe were living in fear following a spate of window-smashing attacks across the town.

When asked whether the revamped MUGA could help reduce anti-social behaviour, Cllr Bailey says he hopes the extra activities would "drift them into the right direction rather than the wrong direction".

He said: "You're never going to stop anti-social behaviour, however, by providing something, you're giving them an option.

"Our biggest problem at the moment is arson which is quite concerning and vandalism has dropped off.

"We all hope as councillors the MUGA gives a much better alternative for them in the town."

To help keep costs down, HTC plans to recycle the existing basketball hoops while repurposing one of the current football goals into a "kick wall".

The facility already benefits from upgraded LED floodlights, allowing it to be used into the evenings.

A dedicated shelter is also expected to be installed nearby, giving young people a place to meet, while climbing structures could also be added if the area proves popular.
